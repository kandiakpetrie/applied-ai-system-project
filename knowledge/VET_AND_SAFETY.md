# Vet Visits and Safety

When to book a vet, and which situations are not schedulable at all.

## Routine vet visits

Healthy adult pets are usually seen once a year for a wellness exam and
vaccines. Puppies, kittens, and senior pets are typically seen more often.

Book routine appointments as one-time tasks with a date; they are not recurring
daily chores.

Withhold food before a procedure only if the clinic asks for it, and follow the
fasting window they give.

## Call a vet the same day

Contact a veterinarian promptly for: refusal to eat for more than 24 hours,
repeated vomiting or diarrhoea, limping that does not improve, a sudden change
in drinking or urination, or unusual lethargy.

These are not tasks to schedule around. They replace the plan for the day.

## Emergencies

Seek emergency care immediately for: difficulty breathing, collapse, seizures,
a suspected broken bone, bloating with retching but no vomit, heat stroke, or
any known poison ingestion.

Keep the number of a 24-hour clinic and a pet poison helpline somewhere easy to
find.

## Common household hazards

Toxic to dogs and cats: chocolate, xylitol, grapes and raisins, onions and
garlic, lilies (severe for cats), antifreeze, and human pain relievers such as
ibuprofen and paracetamol.

Store medication out of reach; a chewed blister pack is an emergency even if
the pet seems fine.

PawPal+ organises care tasks. It does not diagnose illness, choose or change
